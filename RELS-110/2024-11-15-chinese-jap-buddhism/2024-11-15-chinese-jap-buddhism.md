# History
- Buddhist texts/monks arrived in 1st century CE via Silk Road
- Major expansion in China occurs in the 4th-6th centuries CE when it became dominant religion
- Spread from China to Korea in 4th century CE
- Spread from Japan in 6th century CE, where it became de facto national religion
- Mahayama Buddhism is the form that dominates

# Early Buddhism and Daoism
- Shared similarities
- Huayen Buddhism and interdependence: the Jeweled Net of Indra
- World made of emptiness (like world emerging from Dao)
- Monks, like Daoists, living in mountain retreats

# Buddhism Conflicts w/Chinese Culture
Critiques form Confucians:
- Monasticism: threats to filial ties (departing from family, no children), rejection of mendicancy
    - Actually brought in many (like nuns) but monasteries tended to become self-supporting (agriculture)
- Criticized as "foreign" and inimical to Chinese culture
    - Periodic persecutions/outlawing of Buddhism (like 574 CE, 842 BCE)

# Major Schools of Buddhism in China and Japan
China:
- Pureland
- Chan (Zen)
- Tiantai (Tendai)

Japan:
- Pureland
-Zen (Chan)
- Tendai (Tiantai)
- Nichiren

# Pureland
- Most popular Buddhist school in China & Japan
- Introduced in China by Tan Luan (476-542 CE)  as result of vision of Amitabha's Pure Land
- Introduced in Japna by Honen (1133-1212 CE) and his disciple Shinran (1173-1262) known as Jodo Shinshu (true Pureland)

## Amitabha
- Amitofo in Chinese
- Amida in Japanese
- Compassionate Buddha from whose heaven all can become enlightened

# Guanyin
- Chinese name for Avalokitesvara, bodhisattva of compassion and companion to Amitabha
- In China & Japan: a female bodhisattva who especially helps women
- Story of Miaoshan and her great compassion

# Pureland in Practice
- Main practice is chanting the name of Amitabha

Basic Idea:
in addition to "self-power" (your own work towards enlightenment), you need "other-power" (help from Amitabha)
- Individual chanting
    - Namo Amituofo (Chinese)
    - Namo Amida Butsu (Japanese)
- Collective chanting
- 3 or 7 day retreats

Nembutsu: the chant Namo Amida Butsu

# Chan (Zen): Rejects "Other Power" for Self Power
- Don't rely on savior figure
- Doesn't mean obsessive striving for enlightenment; strong influence from Daoism

Similarities to Daoism:
- Both teach that true wisdom doesn't come thru mind, comes thru experience & non-conceptual awareness
    - Emptiness
    - Wu (the hole at the center of the wheel)
- Both traditions say don't exert great effort; instead, learn to live in the moment
    - Sitting meditation/zazen is similar to wu wei
