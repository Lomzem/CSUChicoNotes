# Chinese Medicine Terms
**Qi** (chi): vital energy that makes up everything; circulates through body

**Jing**: "essence", chi in transitionf rom one state to another
    - Example: semen transferring qi from father to fetus
    - Loss of jing from body like semen, menstrual fluid, is major source of qi loss

**Shen** (spirit): yang powered spirit of the body

# Things that Affect Qi
Diet
- Balance of yin/yang foods, not overeating

Exercise
- Qigong and other practices to circulate qi

Blockages
- Herbal medicines (based mostly on yin/yang balance, not chemical compounds)
- Acupuncture
- Acupressure and massage
- Internal Qi manipulation (internal alchemy)

# Wugong (Martial Arts)
- Began as techniques for hunting/self-defense
- Evolved into combat strategies systematized in 7th c. CE
    - Gongfu (Kung fu)
    - Taiji quan (Tai Chi Chuan)
    - Baguazhang

# Internal vs External Martial Arts

## Internal
- Power comes from movements of qi
- Neigong (internal alchemy) at heart of practice (even if practiced by non-Daoists)
- Emphasizes suppleness, yielding, fluidity
- Qigong and Taiji quan

## External
- Power comes from body (tendons, muscles)
