- "Most health beliefs are thought to overlap in three ways; spiritual (sickness from sorcery or evil spirits), balance, (too hot or cold), and a western version of 'germ theory'" (4).

- "Some healing rituals involve chanting, potions, and amulets" (4).

- "For serious illnesses, many times a Buddhist monk is called to pray in the home, and incense is burned" (4)

- "The concept of mental illness does not exist in Vietnamese culture. Depression or other mental health issues are dealt with through spiritual remedies. Typically, Vietnamese people will not use psychologists or psychiatrists for this reason" (4).

- "The Vietnamese concept of balance is a universal force comprised of two essences that harmonize one another. Health beliefs are strongly influenced by forces of hot and cold. Illness is considered to be an imbalance of 'vital forces'" (4).

- "Balance is created by changes in diet when there is too much of one particular element: western medicines, or traditional remedies that involve herbs and medical practices" (4).

# Common Remedies (4-5)

- Coining (Cao gio): "Catch the wind". A coin dipped in mentholated oil is vigorously rubbed across skin. Releases excess force "wind" from body and restores balance

- Cupping (Giac): small heated glasses placed on skin, forming suction that leaves red circular mark, drawing out bad force

- Pinching (Bat gio): Pinching skin allows force to leave the body

- Steaming (Xong): mixture of medicinal herbs is boiled, steal is inhaled, body bathed

- Balm: Medicated oils/balms are rubbed over the skin

- Herbs: Medicinal herbs boiled in water or mixed with alcoholic beverages and consumed. Majority of herbs are not harmful and pose little risk for patients if used properly

- Vietnamese "traditional" medicine practitioners are called Occidental Doctors, or "OD." They are educated from one generation to the next, within families or through apprenticeships. There is no formal training and ODs are men only (5)

- In Vietnam, only the wealthy can afford to go to the MDs in clinics or hospitals (5)

- Oral medication is viewed as 'hot' and is not received well by Vietnamese patients to treat 'hot' illnesses (5)

- They believe that Western medicines i.e. pills will cause more "hotness" to the body thus harm the body in the long run. (5)

- They believe that the Eastern herbs are healthier for the body and resort back to the herbs once the symptoms recur. (5)

- Skin irritation is considered a hot illness, thus a balm or poultice might better meet the patient's desire for balance than a pill in this case. (5)

- Blood loss is concerning, and perceived as a practice that will make one sicker. Hence, surgery is feared. Removal of organs in seen as a last resort, as it alters the internal balance (6)
