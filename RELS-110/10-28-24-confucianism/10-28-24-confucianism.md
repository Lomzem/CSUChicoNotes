# Transliteration
- **Dao** = **Tao**: "way" or "path"
- **Daodejing**: Tao te Ching, important Daoist text
- Qi = Chi: vital energy
- Taiji Quan = Tai Chi Chuan: Daoist martial art

## Names
- Laozi = Lao Tzu, Lao Tsu, Lao Tse: Daoist leader
- Zhuangzi = Chuang Tzu: Daoist thinker
- Kong zi = Confucius
- Mengzi = Mencius
- Xunzi = Hsun Tzu

# Chinese Religion
**syncretism**: blending religions
- Ancient practices
- Confucianism
- Daoism
- Mahayana Buddhism

# Ancient Chinese Religion/Culture
- Importance of ancestors/family
- Rituals of sacrifice to ancestors and gods
- Shamanic practices
- Assumptions about nature of universe
    - Yin/Yang: complementary opposites that make up universe (Dark/light, female/male, moon/sun, etc.)

# Centrality of Family in Chinese Culture
- **Xiao**, filial piety most important cultural virtue
- Reverence for ancestors/parents who gave you life
- Respect/service to elders
- Spelled out in Book of Rites and later Confucian writings

# The Five Relationships
- Ruler and subject
- Parent and child
    - Ancestor/descendent
- Husband and wife
- Elder brother and younger brother
- Friend and friend

# Confucius on Filial Piety
"In serving his parent, a filial son reveres them in daily life; he makes them happy while he nourishes them; he takes anxious care of them in sickness; he shows great sorrow over their death; and he sacrifices to them with solemnity"

# Ancient Shamanism and Chinese Religion
- Shamans who communicated w/spirits through possession trances and oracle bones
- Divinations (oracle bones, I Ching)
- Belief in ghosts/spirits
- Sacrifices to ancestors, gods, nature spirits

# Soul in Chinese Religion
- **Yin soul** (p'o): "animal" soul, stays w/body, can become ghost if body and grave not respected (as many as 7 p'o)
- **Yang soul** (hun): personality, goes to ancestral tablet and/or heavenly realm (as many as 3 hun)
    - religious Taoism and Buddhism believes hun is what gets reincarnated

# Historical Context for Confucianism
- Conflicts among many small territories
- Periods of great empires followed by collapse into smaller warring states
- "Warring states period"

# Life of Confucius (Master Kong)
- Confucius (Kongzi, ca. 551 BCE - 479 BCE)
- Father died at young age
- Literate, mission was to try to educate rulers in best principles of leadership
    - Not very successful in his lifetime
    - Spent time as police commissioner in Lu, putting principles into practice
- Travelled/taught in different regions
- Established school, edited 5 classics producing the Analects (his sayings)

# Confucius's Visual of a Ruler
- Golden age of past: King Yao
- Someone who ruled by power of virtue/moral example: sage kings, whose virtue means kingdom flourishes
- Someone who followed ancient classic texts
    - Ancient history
    - Poetry
    - Divination
    - How to perform rites (li)
    - Music (lost!)

# Junzi
- exemplary person/virtuous person
- "gentleman" / "noble man"
- studies classics and 6 arts
- embodies virtue of **xiao** (filial piety)
- embodies virtue of **ren** (humaneness, benevolence, goodness)
- follows **li** (rituals for good behavior/ceremonial sacrifices)

# Confucius' Rivals: Legalists
- A school said rules should rule w/clear rewards/punishments
    - First to codify/systematize laws
    - Reward virtue, punish crime (corporal punishment, often of your family too)
    - Assumption: need to police behavior
    - Assumption: state has complete control over you

# Qin Dynasty
- 221-206 BCE
- Followed Legalist system
- Conscripted labor to build Great Wall of China
- Did not last long
