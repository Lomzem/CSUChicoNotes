While external and internal martial arts may be similar in some aspects, they are fundamentally different in their goals and their approaches. According to Kerman, "external martial artists power their movements with, not surprisingly, their external strength: tendons, bones, and skin" (Kerman 11). In other words, external martial arts focus on physical strength and the qualities we typically highlight in fights. On the other hand, "the internal arts power their movements with qi, natural energy flow" (Kerman 11). Based on Kerman's descriptions, internal and external martial arts differ sharply in their source of strength.

Internal martial arts are a significant aspect of Daoism because they aid in obtaining qi. Daoists believe that our minds and bodies began united but became separated as we got older. One fundamental goal of Daoism is to obtain qi and to once again unite our minds and bodies. Because internal martial arts prioritize qi in their training, they provide a way to reach that goal of unification. The connection between internal martial arts and qi cultivation is so strong that Kerman defined internal martial art as "any martial art if it also involves neigong, Daoist internal alchemy" (11). In contrast, since external martial arts prioritize physical strength and technique, there is little consideration of qi and internal alchemy.
