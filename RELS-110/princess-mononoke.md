All of the characters in this movie were amazing and they all had their own unique personalities that separated them into special ranks in my mind. San was a human who lived and grew up as a wolf. With a strong mind and heart, she was able to fight for the forest that she called home. She is my favorite character because of the loyalty she had towards the forest. Ashitaka was a strong and selfless guy who was just wanted to protect and save whoever he could during the conflict that was going on in the movie. Personally, he is my 2nd favorite character with his unique ability to fight and try to make peace between everybody in the movie. Even though this character is not on my favorite list, I do think that she is a very well-written character. Lady Eboshi is a strong leader who encourages the people of Iron Town to stand their ground and to never give up on hope. Even though she took away the forest spirit’s head, she did this to help the people of Iron Town so they can make more room for iron mines. She was only doing what she thought was right for her people. She’s a leader of an entire town and that town was her heart. She was willing to do anything to help it grow. Even if it was to kill a spirit. Personally, I think we should be sympathizing with all of the characters, but if I had to choose one, I would say San. The reason why I say this is because her home was being attacked for no reason. They never did any harm to the humans, nor did they create any conflicts, they just simply minded their own business. Her anger towards the humans are completely understandable and justified because they were just minding their own business and then suddenly their home was being destroyed. Another thing I would like to add is that San is human but identifies herself as a wolf. She only grew up around the wolves and the forest, not around humans. She grew up with a negative perspective toward humans because she was abandoned by her parents when she was just a baby.

2) As I mentioned in the lecture, there are both Buddhist and Shinto themes in the film. The two authors for today disagree about the depiction of Buddhism in the film. Dwyer suggests Miyazaki is critical of Buddhism but Chung disagrees; what do you think? How are Shinto and Buddhism depicted in the film?

I align more closely to Chung's perspective that Buddhism is displayed in Princess Mononoke. For example, a crucial component of Buddhism is compassion and non-violence. In the film, Ashitaka is portrayed as trying seek understanding with both humans and spirits even despite being cursed, demonstrating this trait of compassion. Additionally, I really appreciated that Chung highlighted Miyazaki's perspective that "bad karma will convert into constant hatred by revenge." It seems to further support the Buddhist perspective that people should avoid the path of hate and revenge and instead seek a path of compassion and understanding.
