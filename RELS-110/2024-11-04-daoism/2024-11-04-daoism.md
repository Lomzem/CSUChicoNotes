# Daoism
Dao: "way" of nature

# Yin/Yang
- Complementary opposites that make up universe
- Each blends into other
- Balance of them necessary for harmony/health

# History of Daoism
- Laozi (Lao Tzu or Lao Tsu), 500s or 300s BCE
- May have met Confucius
- Maybe archivist for Zhou dynasty who left society "to the west"

# Themes in Daodejing
- Nature of Dao
- How humans should act
- Wu wei: no action, actionless action, going w/flow

## How Rulers Should Lead
- Folow wu wei
- No fixed plans, laws, subsidies
- Explicitly reject principles from Legalism and Confucianism
- Leader best when people barely know he exists

# Daoist Critique of Confucianism
- In pursuit of knowledge, every day something i sadded
- In practice of Tao, every day something is dropped
- Less and less you need to force things until you reach non-action
- When nothing is done, nothing is left undone
- True mastery gained by letting things go their own way
- Can't be gained by interfering

# Philosophical Daoism
- Laozi and Zhuangzi
- Wu-wei: non-action; acting according to nature's rhythms

## Philosophical Daoism and Chinese Life
- Confucianism ordered education, govt, exam process, etc.
- Daoism was counter-cultural force
- Provided balance
- Influenced arts (painting, poetry)
- Influenced ideals connected to nature/recreation

# Religious Daoism
- Combines Daoist teachings w/folk beliefs about gods, spirits, exorcism, etc.

Goals:
- health
- posterity
- prosperity
- immortality

Methods:
Maximizing qi (chi): energy vital force through:
- Diet
- Breath control
- Magical elixirs
- Taiji quan
- Ritual techniques

- Daoist priests assist sick and dead

# Religious Daoist Ritual
Daoist temple tradition emphasizes 3 main types of practice:
1) Doing good deeds
2) Ritual ceremonies
- Offerings to deities (incense, fruit)
- Ask gods for healing, other favors
3) Internal alchemy
- Cultivating qi through chanting, meditation, physical practices
